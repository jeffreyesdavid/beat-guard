How it works

The same core idea Shazam uses, pointed at a different problem.

01 · REGISTER

Fingerprint your beat

The loudest frequency peaks become a "constellation" that survives vocals, compression and noise. register beat.wav

02 · TIMESTAMP

Prove when you made it

Your file's SHA-256 hash is anchored on the Bitcoin blockchain with OpenTimestamps. Only the hash leaves your computer, never the audio.

03 · CHECK

Scan any song

Thousands of matching points that all line up at the same moment can't be a coincidence. It also catches beats sped up or slowed to hide them. check song.mp3

04 · REPORT

Get the evidence

A clean report shows where your beat appears, which part was used, the speed change, and your proof of creation.

What it catches

Tested on beats with vocals on top, every one run through MP3 compression like a real release.

Catches

  • Your beat with vocals on top, even 3× louder than the beat
  • Samples as short as 5 seconds
  • Beats sped up or slowed down by up to 6%, with the exact speed change reported
  • MP3 compression from streaming and uploads

Doesn't catch (yet)

  • A melody re-played from scratch on different instruments
  • Pitch changed without tempo (or tempo without pitch)

Get started

Runs on your own computer with Python 3.9+. Your beats never get uploaded anywhere.

# install
git clone https://github.com/jeffreyesdavid/beat-guard.git
cd beat-guard
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt

# register your beats (export from your DAW as WAV or MP3)
python -m beatguard register ~/Music/beats/*.wav

# check a song you're suspicious about
python -m beatguard check suspect_song.mp3 --report report.html
